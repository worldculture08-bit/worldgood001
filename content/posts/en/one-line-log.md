Most people stop writing a diary for the same reason: the weight of having to fit the whole day in. You sit down in the morning to make sense of everything until evening, you get stuck on the first sentence, you put it off for a few days, and eventually you close the notebook for good.

One line a day removes that weight. You are reducing the day to a single sentence. "The afternoon meeting finished earlier than expected, which was good." "The noodles at lunch were just right." It does not have to be a grand reflection. One line is the minimum stake that holds the day in place.

## What appears once the lines pile up

After about a month of one-liners, something interesting happens. The common pattern of good days starts to show. You notice that your sentences get longer on days when you met someone, or when you finished something. The days they get shorter form a pattern too. A record does not judge you. It just shows you.

Often that is enough on its own. People do not remember their own days accurately. A bad day feels longer than it was, and a good day is forgotten quickly. A one-line record slowly corrects that distortion.

## How to start

If you start today, there is only one method. Before bed, open a note on your phone and leave one sentence. If no sentence comes, write down the first scene that comes to mind. That is all.

A lazy record lasts. A perfect diary ends after one volume; a one-line record lasts ten years.
