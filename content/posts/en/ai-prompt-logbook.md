When you use AI often, you end up repeating the same request. Writing the wording fresh each time makes the results uneven, and it becomes hard to tell why a good one worked. Rather than thinking of prompts as secret orders, treat them as part of the work record, where they are easy to reuse.

## Leave only five boxes

- **Goal**: what were you trying to finish
- **Input**: what material and conditions you put in
- **Request**: in what form you asked the AI
- **Problem**: what was wrong or missing in the result
- **Correction**: what the person finally checked and changed

You do not need to keep only good results. Cases where it produced a wrong number or missed the context also matter — they stop you making the same mistake next time. A short warning like "this prompt needs number checking" is enough.

## Write the work context first

A prompt sentence on its own leaves others unable to tell why it was asked. "Shorten the meeting memo to three lines" is less useful than "organize the meeting memo so that someone who did not attend can see the next actions". Writing the purpose also gives you a standard for judging the result.

That said, do not leave personal data or the original text of confidential material in the record. When necessary, mask names and unique numbers, and separate original text from summary when sharing results. An AI usage record is not a list to show off speed; it is a work note that shows what judgment a person made.

Saving one sentence that came out well does not last as long as leaving one line about why you corrected it. A prompt is a way of using a tool; a correction record is work experience.
