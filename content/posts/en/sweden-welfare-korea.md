When Sweden comes up, the conversation usually goes like this: "They pay high taxes and get welfare in return." And then it moves straight to "why can't we do that too." I have said it myself. But at some point I started wondering: how did they get there? Just because their citizens are nice?

## The premises of the Swedish model

The Nordic model, Sweden included, rests on high taxes, high social trust, strong labour-management cooperation, a small population and a stable industrial structure. If any one of those is missing, the welfare built on top of it wobbles quickly. Let me point out one thing everyone already knows. **They did not become this overnight.** It is the result of nearly a century of labour and management colliding, compromising and rewriting the rules.

That is also why it cannot be applied to Korea as is. Population size differs, industrial roots differ, the history of labour relations differs. A welfare system is not an apple you transplant and plant — it is something grown to fit the soil.

## There is still something to learn

Not being able to copy it is not a reason to give up. What is worth learning from Sweden is not the tax number but the attitude. First, a culture where labour and management treat each other as partners. Second, investment that does not skimp on children and care. Third, a way of seeing welfare not as a tool of political fights but as **basic infrastructure of society**. These three are not statutory clauses but matters of disposition, which means we can start too.

One more thing. Sweden is not perfect these days either. It is struggling with immigration, integration and youth housing. Rather than searching for a perfect country and ending up able to do nothing, **learning what there is to learn and translating it into our own terms** is the wiser path.

I would like to see a welfare model with a Korean name one day. Not "the Swedish model," but one shaped by the voice of our own workplaces. That is what real learning means.
