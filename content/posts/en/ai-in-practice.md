Talk about AI for long and it turns grand quickly. Automation, innovation, replacement… On site, what matters first is **what to do today**. So I use AI less as a "clever assistant" and more as **a tool that reduces repetitive work**. The name of a tool may be flashy, but its use has to stay plain to last.

## Three things it attaches to well

1. **Drafting** — removing the blank screen, producing material for me to correct quickly
2. **Summarizing and organizing** — turning long notes, meeting content and material into structure
3. **Polishing expression** — fixing rough sentences to read better (careful not to change the facts)

These three share one thing: the quality of the input is the quality of the output. A note thrown in carelessly is organized carelessly. In return, a note that separates fact, utterance and interpretation is easy for the AI to structure and easy for me to verify.

## Ways not to use it

- Writing unverified numbers as they are
- Letting the AI rewrite what a person on site said into a sentence it invented
- Avoiding responsibility with "the AI said so"
- Pasting sensitive personal information in as it is

A tool raises your speed, but **judgment and responsibility stay with the person**. The more you use AI, the more carefully you read and the more briefly you verify. In public posts especially, ask once more at the end: "is the basis of this sentence in my notes?"

## Start with small experiments

One thing a day: structure a meeting note and look at it, draft an email, pull out tag candidates. Practice changes when the record of small experiments piles up, not with a grand adoption plan. Each time you experiment, leave one line on "what did this save / what did I nearly miss". Those lines become the team's AI usage guide.

AI does not replace the site. It helps leave it more clearly. That, at least, suits the way I work.
