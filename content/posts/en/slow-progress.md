There are periods in work with no visible result. You repeat something similar every day, the numbers stay the same, and the world around you seems to be moving fast. Most people either change direction or stop in that period.

Looking back, though, most of the things that lasted were made in that boring stretch. Trust with people, understanding of the site, the accumulation of documents — none of that is built in a day or two. While it was invisible, it was still piling up a little every day.

## Progress is a staircase

Progress is not a line but a staircase. Long stretches that look like no climbing at all, then suddenly you are up one step. The problem is that you cannot know the shape of the stairs in advance. So to judge whether you are climbing now, look at the direction rather than the result.

Checking the direction is simple: put last month's record next to this month's. Day by day, it looks like nothing changed; month by month, the sentences have quietly firmed up. That is why a record is needed. A record is the only evidence of slow progress.

## Waiting is also work

Waiting is not passive time. Continuing while holding the direction takes more energy than people expect. Keeping your own pace while the world looks fast is itself something you have to train.

On days with no fast result, the record still gets written. Once those records pile up, they become the foothold for the day you climb one step. Believing in slow progress comes down, in the end, to believing in the record.
