Year-end settlement is not a "13th month salary." It is the **settlement of a full year of tax**. What you get back is neither large nor complicated. Remember the rate and the condition for each item and you are done. Some items reduce taxable income (income deduction), others reduce tax directly (tax credit). That difference decides how much you actually save.

## Item-by-item comparison

| Item | Type | Rate | Key condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Credit card and similar spending | Income deduction | 15% of the amount over 25% of total pay | Cash receipts and direct debit are 30%; traditional markets and public transport may qualify for more |
| Medical expenses | Tax credit | 15% | Amount above 3% of total pay, self or dependants |
| Education | Tax credit | 15% | Kindergarten through university tuition; academy fees are limited |
| Donations | Tax credit | 15% (30% above 30 million won) | Amount over 100,000 won, carryable for 10 years |
| Rent | Tax credit | 10% (12% if conditions met) | Total pay and housing conditions met, 7.5 million won annual cap |
| Guaranteed insurance premiums | Tax credit | 12% | 1 million won annual cap |
| Pension savings | Income deduction | 15% | 4 million won annual cap (7 million with IRP combined) |
| Housing loan interest | Income deduction | Within the cap | Housing and income conditions |

## Income deduction vs tax credit

One million won is not worth the same in each case.

- **Income deduction**: subtract 1 million won from taxable income. Actual saving = 1 million × your marginal rate (6–45%).
- **Tax credit**: subtract 1 million won straight from the tax owed. Saving = 1 million won.

For an ordinary worker (taxable income up to 100 million won) tax credit items give a larger real saving. That is why it matters not to miss items needing documentation — rent, medical expenses, donations.

## Worked example — 40 million won salary

Salary 40 million won, 18 million on credit cards, 2 million in cash receipts.

1. Threshold = 25% of total pay = 10 million won
2. Excess = 20 million − 10 million = 10 million won
3. Card portion 8 million × 15% = 1.2 million; cash portion 2 million × 30% = 0.6 million
4. Total income deduction = 1.8 million won

If this person's comprehensive income tax rate sits in the 15% band, the saving is 1.8 million × 15% = **about 270,000 won**.

Under the same conditions, a tax credit on 6 million won of rent (within the 7.5 million cap) is 6 million × 10% = **600,000 won**. In other words, collecting rent documentation pays better than pushing card spending higher.

## Four common mistakes

- **Dependant documents not submitted** — even if you paid for a parent's medical care, no deduction without the dependency conditions and paperwork.
- **Cash receipt not issued** — spending without a receipt drops out of the deduction.
- **Missing rent evidence** — you need the lease, the transfer record and the resident registration extract.
- **Donation receipts left until December** — if you do not collect them during the year, they are hard to find.

## Reference date and caveats

This post reflects the general structure as of October 2026. Tax rules change every year and some deductions have sunset clauses. Before filing, check your own numbers with the National Tax Service year-end settlement simplified service and the estimate calculator.
