When a record rests for a few days, guilt attaches. The moment you think "I have to write all of it up", you let go of the pen again. **Restarting** is closer to the answer than recovering. Lose the present while filling the blanks of the past, and the habit ends.

## The restart protocol

1. Acknowledge the gap in one line only ("three days off")
2. Pick a single scene from today
3. Write it with the three-line template
4. Decide tomorrow whether to publish

The heart of this order is acknowledge → shrink → do → defer. Without acknowledgement, guilt blocks the writing; without shrinking, the weight blocks it. Doing is short; publishing can wait. The private note comes first, publishing later.

## Devices that prevent the backlog

Put a short writing block in the calendar, fix the template, and cut your tools down to one. Prevention does not need to be perfect. It is enough that the road back is visible when you collapse. That road is the restart protocol.

The field is new every day. One line today is the material for tomorrow. If you have a backlog, write three lines below this paragraph, right now. That is enough. There is no such thing as too late a morning to start again.

## The relief that comes after restarting

Once you write the three lines, the whole backlog is not resolved. What comes back instead is the identity of "someone who writes again". That identity is the fuel of the habit. In the long run, an identity that does not break produces more posts than a perfect archive.

When someone asks "you have not been writing lately?", do not defend yourself. Answer only: "starting again today". A record is not a report card. It is a tool.
