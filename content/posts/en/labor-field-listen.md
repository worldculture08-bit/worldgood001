On many sites the answer already exists. It is just not in a document — it sits in people's words, habits and fatigue. So the first step of planning is often **listening**. If you skip the listening and arrive with a solution, even a good intention can feel like interference.

## The basic posture of listening

- Do not toss out a solution mid-way
- Do not push with "so what is the conclusion?"
- Check the same statement once more from a different angle
- Repeat briefly what you heard and ask if it is right
- Do not rush the silence

Listening is not passive. The real talk only comes out when the other person feels "this one actually listens". On a labour site, whether speaking up is even safe is itself a question of power. Who listens, who writes it down, and where that record goes are all questions of safety.

## Listening that leaves a record

If you do not organise it immediately, what you heard turns into your own interpretation. Divide the columns in your site notes into "what the person said / what I felt", which reduces distortion later when you write it up. Where possible, keep the key sentences in the other person's own words. Polishing comes afterwards.

Always give the result of listening back as feedback. "This is how I understood it — is that right?" opens the door to the next conversation. Listening you never return is close to collecting; listening you return becomes a relationship.

Stories about labour and work begin not with a grand slogan but with **one person's day**. Listening that respects that day is where a record starts. In one conversation today, try confirming once more instead of solving.
