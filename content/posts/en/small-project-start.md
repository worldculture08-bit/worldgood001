Small work looks like it will finish quickly, so the plan gets skipped. But if the end of something you started is not fixed, small work keeps taking up space in your head. I divide tasks into three sections: start, middle, end.

## Start: define the shape of the end

"Look into starting a business" is too big. Define the deliverable instead, as in "this week, check the licences and contact details of three candidates and put them in a table." What you need is a criterion that lets you say this stage is done — not perfect research.

## Middle: report the blockage early

At the middle check, do not embellish the progress rate. Look at three things only: what is confirmed, what is not confirmed yet, and what needs the plan changed. If you hide a blockage and mention it on the deadline, your options shrink. Share it while the problem is still small and there is time to try another approach.

## End: close the result and leave a next step

The end is not the moment the file exists. It is the moment the result is delivered and the next decision is written down. One line each for: who you sent what to, which items are on hold, and the first action if you started again.

This is not a large project management method forced onto small work. It is the minimum rhythm of narrowing the purpose at the start, reporting the blockage in the middle, and leaving what you learned at the end. The smaller the work, the better this rhythm fits without becoming a burden.
