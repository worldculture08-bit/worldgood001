Search for work AI automation and a tool list comes up first. But automation stops on site not because tools are missing. It stops because you **choose the tool before deciding what to automate and where a person still judges**. Tools can be decided last.

## Step 1: write down repetitive work with its time

Do not pick automation candidates by instinct. Write down the work repeated over a week and record these four things with it:

- How often it repeats
- How long each time takes
- Who it affects if it goes wrong
- Where the input material lives

Without those four you cannot distinguish "automation that looks good" from "automation that actually saves time". The larger **frequency × time each × cost of error**, the earlier it deserves review.

## Step 2: split the work into input, judgment and delivery

Break one task into three parts:

- Input: gathering material and matching formats
- Judgment: setting priority and taking responsibility
- Delivery: sharing results and recording them

Input and drafting fit automation well. Judgment stays with the person. When organizing meeting notes, what you can hand to the AI is **rearranging utterances in time order and separating decisions from open questions**. Deciding who is responsible for what is a person's job.

Without that distinction, a plausible sentence from the AI stays standing as a real decision. The risk then is not speed but a gap in responsibility.

## Step 3: choose the smallest tool that works

Go down this order when choosing an automation tool:

1. Is it possible with document or spreadsheet features you already use
2. Can it be reduced to a fixed format and template
3. Can an AI draft take over something one person repeats
4. Only consider a separate tool when several people and systems must connect

The bigger the tool, the larger the setup, training and maintenance cost. Attaching a large system to work a small tool handles adds an administrative job instead of automation.

## Step 4: test one person, one task, two weeks

Do not apply it to the whole team at the start. One person tests one task for two weeks. Set the criteria before testing:

- Time with the old method
- Time with the tested method
- Whether errors went down or up
- Whether it is easy to reverse

Ending with "it feels easier" and no criteria leaves you with no ground to persuade the next person. With criteria, even a failure teaches something.

Separate sensitive material before testing. Customer names, contact details, contract terms and health information must not go outside, so exclude them from AI input or anonymize them. The criteria are in [handling personal data in AI work](/en/p/privacy-in-ai-work).

## Step 5: attach verification and a record

Automated results pass through a verification step before use.

- Do numbers and dates match the original
- Are names and titles unchanged
- Is anything inferred written as fact
- Is the final decision-maker recorded

The verification rule does not have to be long. One line is enough: "compare numbers and proper nouns with the original before use." That line prevents a lot of accidents.

After verification passes, record the **process** rather than the result. What input you used, what was automated and what a person judged — that can be reused as it is in the next task. See [a work note you can find again needs five boxes](/en/p/work-note-template).

## Questions to ask before automating

- Does this task really repeat often
- Did you split what to automate from what a person judges
- Can you reverse it if it fails
- Is there a named person who checks the result
- Is there a criterion for judging success or failure after two weeks

The purpose of work AI automation is not to remove people but to give people time for the judgments that matter more. Fix the order before the tool and automation lasts.
