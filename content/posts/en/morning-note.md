The morning note is not a grand diary. While you sit down and pour your coffee, you open a notebook and write three lines: the person you will meet today, the one thing you must finish today, the thing you do not have to do today. Those three lines quietly set the direction of the day.

A day turns to chaos not usually because there is too much to do, but because you start without knowing what comes first. If you decide the order in the morning, the noise of the afternoon gets smaller. So does the habit of checking the same thing twice.

## Why the same time

Habits come from place, not from willpower. Same time, same place, same tool. The mornings when you open social media while the notebook lies open are different in density from the mornings when you do not. Writing three lines takes three minutes. Those three minutes create room for the morning.

Trying to make it perfect means it will not last. Some days three lines become one, and some days you leave a mark on your calendar instead of a note. That is fine. What matters is not the length of the record but asking yourself for a direction as you open the day.

## A record is a promise to yourself

Three lines in the morning are a short promise to your future self. If you look at the notebook again around lunchtime, you can see what the morning version of you considered important. When your judgment wavers, that is one standard gained.

Let us try starting with three lines today too. It lasts because it is not grand.
