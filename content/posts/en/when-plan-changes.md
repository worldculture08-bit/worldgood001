Plans change often on site. Material does not arrive, an approval is late, an unforeseen condition appears. The bigger problem than the plan changing is that the people affected find out late.

## Decide the order of sharing first

When announcing a change, look at the size of the impact before rank or closeness. Tell first the people who must stop or move work immediately, and those who have to rearrange outside commitments. Then share the current situation and the options with the decision-makers and collaborators.

A notice should carry five things.

- What changed
- Why it changed
- Who is affected, and how
- What alternatives are possible right now
- By when you will have an answer

When the cause is unknown, do not force a conclusion. Mark the scope with "as far as is currently confirmed" and write down when you will check again. Hiding uncertainty means losing trust later, when the change grows wider.

## A short change notice

> Today's afternoon work has a new start time because we are confirming material. Site crew, please wait until 2 p.m.; the person in charge will post an update on arrival by noon. If confirmation is late, we will consider moving to tomorrow morning.

A notice like this is not a perfect plan document. It only says what is different now and when the next decision comes. The habit of sharing changes quickly and without hiding them is the sturdiest safety net on a site where plans keep wobbling.
