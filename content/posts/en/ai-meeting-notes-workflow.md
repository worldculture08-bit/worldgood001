Throw "summarize this memo" at the AI after a meeting and you get back a readable paragraph. But that paragraph easily lacks **who agreed to do what**. The purpose of meeting notes is not the summary; it is leaving the next action. So the order matters.

## Step 1: Collect — facts only, no judgment

During the meeting do not try to organize conclusions. Instead, write down these separately:

- Confirmed fact: documents, numbers, schedules, states seen directly
- Utterance: who offered which opinion
- Decision: what is settled right now
- Undecided: questions still unanswered
- Next action: who does what by when

Without these five boxes, the AI fills the blanks with plausible sentences. Just not mixing fact and judgment during the meeting already changes the quality of the summary considerably.

One more important point is consent and scope. If a meeting needs recording or an external AI tool, tell attendees in advance and leave sensitive items out of the input. Check the standard for personal data and internal material first in [handling personal data in AI work](/en/p/privacy-in-ai-work).

## Step 2: Structure — give the AI both a role and prohibitions

When handing over notes to the AI, do not state only the purpose. Attach these four things:

```text
Purpose: a summary to share with meeting attendees
Reader: colleagues who took part in the meeting
Prohibitions: no invention, no fabricated numbers, do not anonymize names, do not create decisions that were not made
Format: decisions / next actions / open questions / shared items
```

And have it mark anything not in the original as "needs confirmation". Without that one line, the AI connects the context naturally and can invent decisions that were never made. Prompt writing is covered further in [field prompts](/en/p/ai-prompt-field).

## Step 3: Verify — separate decisions from opinions again

Do not share the AI's structured result as it is. Check four things:

1. **Decisions**: was this actually agreed, or is it one person's proposal
2. **Next actions**: is there an owner and a deadline, or does it end in a vague verb like "review"
3. **Numbers and dates**: do they match the original notes
4. **Names and titles**: have they changed from reality

For "next actions" in particular, rewrite so they start with a verb. "Send a comparison table of two budget options by Friday" is easier to judge as done than "review the budget".

## Step 4: Deliver — let the reader move immediately

Shared meeting notes do not have to be long. This order is enough:

- One-line conclusion
- Decisions
- Next actions with owners
- Open questions
- Material to check before the next meeting

When you deliver, saying "compared against the original and confirmed" protects trust better than "the AI organized this". AI is a drafting tool, and the final responsibility belongs to whoever chaired the meeting.

## Review checklist for meeting notes

- Are fact, utterance, decision and open question separated
- Is no AI invention left standing as fact
- Do next actions have an owner and a deadline
- Is no sensitive information left in
- Did you compare against the original notes

The value of automating meeting notes should be measured not by "it was organized fast" but by "at the next meeting we can check what happened last time". With that standard, AI becomes a link in the work rather than a summarizer.
