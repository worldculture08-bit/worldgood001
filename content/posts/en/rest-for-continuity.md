The more there is to do, the more rest feels like a waste. Yet sitting on with an empty body and empty focus means rereading the same sentence and spending time on small mistakes. Stopping is not giving up on the work; it is preparing the next start.

## Three lines when you stop

At the end of the day, leave these three lines:

1. How far did I get today
2. What question is still open
3. What is the one thing I do first when I sit down next time

The last line is the important one. "Continue tomorrow" gives a high threshold; "check the two empty cells in the comparison table at 9 a.m." lowers it. Resting while carrying the work in your head stops the body but keeps the brain on standby.

## Rest needs a boundary too

During rest I try not to add new work. If I have to check messages, I set a time for it, and anything that does not need an answer now moves to the next working period. People who say when they can answer last longer than people who answer everything immediately.

Consistency is not the ability to do the same amount every day. It is the ability to lower the pace to match the situation without losing the road back to starting. If today's record points to tomorrow's first action, you stopped well enough.
