There are weeks when nothing happens. No big meeting, no big decision, no special event. Go to work, work, come home — seven times over. Looking back after such a week leaves you feeling hollow.

But was it really nothing? If you look closely at a day, small changes were there every day. The day you finally organized the paperwork you kept postponing, a remark a colleague made, the view through the window at lunch. The problem is that these disappear from memory once the day passes. Not because there were no events, but because nothing was kept.

## The device of a weekly record

The way to get through a quiet week is a weekly record. On Friday afternoon, take ten minutes to pick three scenes worth keeping from the week: one thing you did well, one thing you learned, one thing to carry into next week. Three is enough.

Once you write it, something strange happens. The "week where nothing happened" becomes "a week that moved a little". It is the same week, but the moment a record is attached the density changes.

## Quiet is not a bad signal

Quiet periods at work are usually periods when preparation accumulates. Even with no visible movement, relationships deepen and understanding grows. If you count only visible events as performance, this kind of time is ignored forever.

You can still record in a quiet week. In fact, quiet weeks need records more. A noisy week stays in memory on its own, but a quiet week passes without a trace unless you leave something. Those traces pile up and become a year.
