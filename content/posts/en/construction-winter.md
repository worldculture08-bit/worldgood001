If I had to name one indicator that reports the economy earliest, it would be **the number of cranes standing against the sky**. In the busy years, wherever you went there were cranes queued in a line. These days it is different. What stands out is a handful of cranes visible in the distance, standing in the same place for a long time. Cranes make noise when they move, and the sky is quiet now.

Where construction goes quiet, the noise appears somewhere else.

## Why this is not someone else's problem

Construction does not run on its own. Cement, steel, equipment, transport, design, interiors — it is a chain. When one site stops, dozens of jobs behind it stop slowly. The larger construction's share of the local economy, the bigger the effect. Restaurants, hardware shops and moving centres all feel it.

The numbers hurt more. **You build a building, it does not sell; it does not sell, no new orders come in.** Material and labour costs rise while contract prices cannot, so companies choose to cut orders. Fewer orders eventually means fewer people. The people I met on site know this sequence in their bodies.

## Why worry alone is not a plan

This is not an argument for standing still. Winter exists; the problem is how you endure it. I watch three things.

First, investment in safety and quality must not be cut. When orders fall, the safety budget is usually the first thing trimmed, and that is the most expensive saving available. Second, companies that retrain rather than cut people win in the next cycle. Third, policy has to build **a sustainable rhythm of work**, not a one-off boost. SOC investment, ageing infrastructure renewal and green remodelling are that rhythm.

## The people preparing for spring

Foremen do more in winter. They clean equipment, read drawings, study for qualifications. They do not wait it out doing nothing. **The longer the winter, the sooner the person who prepared meets spring.** Industry and people look the same.

Listening to the quiet of the cranes, I hope the quiet is a comma, not an ending. Writing the sentence that comes after that comma is our work.
