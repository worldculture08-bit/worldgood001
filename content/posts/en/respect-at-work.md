Work requires training in speaking in numbers. But if you only speak in numbers, people disappear. A site record is also a place to practise that balance. This is not an argument against results. It is an argument for looking at the conditions that produced them.

## Expressions to be careful with

- Calling people only by their role
- Reducing a failure to a personality
- Filing a complaint you heard as mere "grievance"
- Packaging speed as a moral virtue

Respect is not decoration. It is closer to **accurate description**: who did what, under what conditions. Once those conditions show, the reader and your future self both judge people more fairly. Sometimes "how many hours was it, and what was missing" respects a person more than the word "hardworking".

## Write results and dignity together

Try adding a single line of conditions below your results paragraph. Resources, manpower, schedule, safety. With that one line, the same number reads differently. In reverse, a record left with numbers and no conditions is easily reused later as a tool for pressuring people.

Results matter. But if you erase the grain of the day that produced them, the sense for designing the next result disappears with it. Writing from the field has a part in protecting that sense.

## The question left on the site

Ask yourself whether the sentence you wrote today reads a person as a tool. Even describing the same fact, "who failed" and "what was missing" have a different grain. The second one opens the next design.
