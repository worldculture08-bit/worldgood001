The more technical terms you use, the deeper it looks. But the reader on site is busy. Commute, rest break, between meetings — your post has to be readable in those short gaps. Difficult writing is sometimes not a sign of depth but a cover for thoughts that were never sorted.

## Practising simple writing

- One idea per sentence
- One concrete scene next to each abstract word
- An action actually taken instead of "shall be" or "is"
- One thing the reader can use tomorrow, at the end of the paragraph
- Foreign words and acronyms written out once, only when needed

Simple sentences are not shallow thoughts. They are **organised thoughts**. Stories about labour and sites travel further precisely because they are simple. A post only connects to work when reading it brings up "the next action I can take."

## Editing with respect for the reader

Not saying the same thing twice, getting numbers and dates right, not caricaturing people. Editing is not sentence decoration; it is courtesy. The busier the reader, the more their time you steal, the more cutting filler is an act of respect.

I try to hold this blog to the same standard. Fewer hard words, and evidence in what remains. If the reader's hand moves a little after reading, that post has done its job.

## Keeping the language of the site alive

Polish what you heard on site too much and the vividness goes. That does not mean copying profanity verbatim. It does mean keeping the other person's metaphors and rhythm where you can. "I'm exhausted" says less than "my feet clocked off before I did."

Easy writing gets easier when it respects how people on site talk. It needs an editor who places the site's own sentences accurately rather than papering over them with the writer's voice.
