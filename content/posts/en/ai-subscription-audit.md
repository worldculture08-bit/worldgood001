AI and SaaS tools grow quietly once adopted. When each team member pays on their own, you pay twice for the same feature; seats of people who left remain; forgotten auto-renewals fire. An audit **once a quarter**, on **one page**, removes a large part of this.

## Why things overlap

Tools grow through three paths.

1. **Individual payment** — a team member pays with a personal card for work
2. **Department payment** — a department contracts because it needs the tool
3. **Bundle** — the feature is included in a higher tier of an existing tool

When the three combine, the list itself disappears. The first step of an audit is **making the list**.

## Step 1: build the full list

Pull the subscriptions from the last 12 months out of your payment records into a table.

| Tool | Purpose | Payer | Seats | Monthly cost | Renewal date | Actual use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (e.g.) summary AI | Document summary | Team | 10 | — | 5th monthly | 3 people |
| (e.g.) meeting AI | Minutes | Department | 5 | — | 20th monthly | 5 people |

Merge card statements, invoices and app-store payments. **Filling in the "actual use" column** is the key.

## Step 2: find duplicate features

Group tools that do the same thing.

- Document summary: tool A and tool B
- Minutes: tool C and the built-in feature of the video meeting app
- Translation: tool D and the built-in feature of the document app

There is often a separate payment for something already in a bundle. Keep one and cancel the rest. Which one to keep is decided by [7 criteria for choosing AI and SaaS tools](/en/p/ai-tool-selection-scorecard).

## Step 3: clean up seats

Check whether "number of seats = number of actual users".

- Seats of people who left or moved
- Accounts barely used
- Positions that a shared account could cover

Do this at least once a quarter. One seat is one monthly payment.

## Step 4: check the plan tier

See whether you actually use what the higher tier offers.

- Admin features: do you use them
- Advanced features: how many people use them
- Usage limits: do you often exceed them or never approach them

If you hit the limit often, move up; if you never approach it, move down. The boundary between free and paid is covered in [free vs paid AI plans](/en/p/ai-plan-free-vs-paid).

## Step 5: manage renewal dates and ceilings

Auto-renewal is convenient and leaks quietly.

- Put all renewal dates in one calendar
- Set a review reminder **two weeks before** renewal
- Set a monthly ceiling on usage-based plans
- Gather payment responsibility in one person

## Step 6: re-audit after three months

An audit does not end once. Record these items and compare three months later.

- Number of tools
- Total monthly cost
- Cost per person
- Actual usage rate

If the number of tools went down but the total did not, the problem is the **tier**, not the overlap.

## Common mistakes

- Not gathering payment records in one place
- Paying separately for features included in a bundle
- Keeping tools "just in case" that nobody uses
- Ignoring seats of people who left
- Not putting renewal dates in a calendar

## Checklist

- [ ] Built the full 12-month subscription list
- [ ] Filled in actual users
- [ ] Found duplicate features and kept only one
- [ ] Cleaned up seats
- [ ] Checked plan tiers
- [ ] Sorted out renewal dates and ceilings
- [ ] Set a date for the next audit in three months
