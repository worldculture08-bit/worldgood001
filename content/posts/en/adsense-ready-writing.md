When building a blog, it is easy to think about ads first. Wanting revenue is natural, but filling the ad slots first does not make readers appear. People come back because of what they take away after reading, not because of the ads.

## One kind of help per post

Before writing, decide what question the reader arrives with. If the post explains a site briefing, focus on one thing — what has to be lined up in a short time — rather than covering every problem of the meeting. Promise that help in the title and the first sentence, then show the real order and examples in the body.

When writing about experience, separate what you saw yourself from the method you generally recommend. When writing about someone else's story, do not reveal identity unnecessarily and cover only what may be published. Readable sentences and a precise scope last longer than fancy wording.

## Put the ads after the content

Place ads where they do not interrupt the flow, and make the post complete its purpose without them. If ad setup is not ready yet, rather than over-decorating an empty slot, prepare an introduction, a contact page and a privacy notice so readers can understand the site first.

Even when writing for search, do not take one post and rephrase it slightly into several. Putting real experience, mistakes and criteria to check into a single post is harder, but it is the more honest method. While you wait for ad approval, the quality of the posts and the rhythm of your record keep building.

Revenue is the result. The help the reader gets is the starting point. That is the order I want to keep.
