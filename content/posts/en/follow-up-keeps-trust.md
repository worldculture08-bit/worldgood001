Everyone says nice things when they first meet. The problem starts after the promised work begins. If there is no contact, the other side cannot tell whether the work is in progress, has stopped, or whether they have been forgotten. For someone waiting, silence is not information — it is anxiety.

A follow-up is not something to do only after a big result. You should report the current state even when there is no result yet. Do not stop at "I'm checking on it" — write what you checked, where you got stuck, and when you will report again.

## Three sentences are enough

A progress update can start with three sentences:

1. What you have confirmed so far
2. What is still unresolved
3. What you will do next, and when you will be in touch again

For example: "I've checked two quotes and I'm waiting on one schedule. I'll send a comparison table by tomorrow morning, and if they don't answer I'll suggest other dates as well." It does not have to be good news to become the information someone needs for the next decision.

## Reduce promises you cannot keep

Trying to keep trust by promising a fast date no matter what usually makes things worse. It is better to say what you do not know yet and offer a deadline you can actually verify. The most realistic way to keep a promise is to make it smaller.

A follow-up is not the technique of maintaining a relationship. It is handing over information so that the people you work with can make the next decision. Even when the result is delayed, if the contact continues, people can tell the difference between the problem itself and the failure to communicate.
