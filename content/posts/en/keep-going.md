Watching people who work for years, you notice a common thing. They are not people with unusually strong willpower. They are people who have a separate rule for stopping: what to do and what not to do when they are sick, when they are busy, and when they do not feel like it.

The force to continue is not a muscle you build by enduring. It is a structure. A structure that leaves one line a day, a structure that organizes once a week. With a structure in place, a minimum trace remains even when your condition is off.

## Set a minimum version

If you build a habit against the standard of a good day, it collapses. So set a minimum version. The minimum version of a daily record is one line, of a weekly review five minutes, of reaching out a single greeting text. If you keep only the minimum version, that day is not a failure.

The minimum version matters because it lowers the cost of restarting. After skipping a day, you need mental preparation to start again. But if you kept the minimum version, it is not starting, it is continuing. Continuing takes less courage.

## What a record leaves behind

On site and in writing alike, what remains in the end is a trace. Not one perfect instance, but dots that connected without being chosen. A record keeps those dots. Dots become a line, and a line becomes a direction.

The people who continue are not special. They are people with a structure that does not stop. Today's one line is the first brick of that structure.
