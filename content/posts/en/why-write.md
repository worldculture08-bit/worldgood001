Stories you hear on site scatter easily. They stay for a while in meeting notes, in chat messages, and in your head, and then they are gone. The scene you were certain about becomes "so what was the story again?" within days. The more your role connects people and work, the more that gap costs you — because you repeat the same mistake, repeat the same explanation, and rebuild the same trust from zero.

So I started **harugirok**. It is not a grand magazine. It is closer to a notebook where what I heard and felt is quietly organized and kept. I think **a record left behind** matters more than a perfect sentence. It is not a place to become someone who writes well; it is closer to a place for not losing what was learned on site.

## Without a record you repeat the same mistake

Planning that connects people and work does not finish in one go. Similar situations and similar conflicts come back with different faces. If you have no material to answer "how did we handle this last time?", you end up relying on feeling alone. Feeling is an asset, but feeling alone cannot be passed to someone else. Even a short post becomes a clue for the next choice and a bridge for handing context to a colleague.

On site there is often an illusion that "everyone knows this". In reality only a few people know, and most hold fragments. A record joins those fragments. One agreed sentence from a meeting, one sentence overheard on site, one thing to try next time — leaving just those changes the following week.

## What I want to do here

- Put down briefly the points where site work and people meet
- Organize what I learn in work and planning
- Write honestly about how tools, AI included, were attached to practice
- Write about scenes where the dignity of work and people is visible, without exaggeration
- Build records that can be unfolded again later

I keep to learning and organizing slowly on site rather than declaring myself an expert. I may be wrong, and my thinking may change later. Even so, leaving the evidence and the interpretation separated means that even a changed mind becomes an asset.

## The smallest unit of writing

I do not promise a grand series. Instead, on days I can write, I write at the length I can manage. Some days it is three lines, some days it runs long. What matters is not treating a blank page as something to fear. A blank page is not failure; it is a site you have not moved yet.

I would be glad if readers find a small clue or a moment of agreement here. Today you can start with one line. When those lines pile up, they become a map left by work and by people.
