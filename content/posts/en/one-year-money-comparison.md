One year is too short to call it investing. Over a short horizon, what decides the outcome is not raising the yield but **keeping the term** and **cutting tax and fees**. Let's take 30 million won for one year and work through the products.

## The basic formula

After-tax return = principal × rate × term − (interest × 15.4%)

Interest from deposits, savings plans and government bonds is subject to 15.4% separate taxation. That is why you compare rates after tax, not before. (If financial income exceeds 20 million won a year it becomes a separate comprehensive taxation question, but at the 30 million won level it usually does not apply.)

## Product comparison (30 million won principal, example rates)

| Product | Example rate | Pre-tax interest | After tax | Liquidity | Principal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-year time deposit | 2.8%/yr | 840,000 won | about 710,000 won | Medium (early withdrawal cuts interest) | Deposit protection to 50 million won |
| Regular savings plan (2.5M × 12) | 3.0%/yr | about 490,000 won | about 410,000 won | High (monthly deposits) | Deposit protection |
| 1-year treasury bond (direct) | 2.7%/yr | 810,000 won | about 680,000 won | High (tradable) | Essentially none |
| Inflation-linked treasury bond | Variable | Tracks inflation | Tracks inflation | Medium | Rate and inflation change |
| Bond fund | Variable | After fees | Variable | High | Price change |
| CMA (MMF) | 2.5%/yr | 750,000 won | about 630,000 won | Very high | Low |

The rates are examples. Check the rates a bank or broker publishes at the time you open the account.

## The hidden calculation — why savings plans earn less

A savings plan rate (3.0%) is higher than a deposit (2.8%), yet the interest is smaller. Because you deposit monthly, the average time on deposit is only about half.

- Total deposited: 2.5 million × 12 = 30 million won
- Simple approximation: interest ≈ monthly deposit × (annual rate ÷ 12) × (1 + 2 + ... + 12)
- = 2.5 million × 0.0025 × 78 = **about 488,000 won pre-tax**

Since the 30 million won was not on deposit for the full year, the actual return against principal is around 1.6% a year. A savings plan is not a "higher rate" product — it is a device that forces you to save every month. If you already have the lump sum, deposits or bonds are better on paper.

## Four criteria for choosing

1. **Chance of early withdrawal** — money you may spend within the year is not for a deposit. CMA or a cash account is the right answer. An emergency fund is not a return target.
2. **Deposit protection limit** — principal plus interest is protected up to 50 million won per bank. Above that, split it.
3. **Fixed or variable rate** — a 1-year deposit locks the rate at signup. Variable products fall with the benchmark.
4. **Compound interest illusion** — most 1-year products pay simple interest. There is effectively no compounding over a one-year window.

## Choose for your own situation

- **Money spent exactly one year later (moving, tuition)** — a time deposit or 1-year treasury bond maturing when you need it.
- **Spare money appearing each month** — a savings plan to force the habit, then move it into a deposit.
- **Money you cannot date** — CMA or a cash account. Trade yield for liquidity.

## Summary

Across these products, the after-tax difference over a year is within roughly 100,000 won on 30 million won. Rather than spend time narrowing that gap, it pays more to build a structure that will not be broken early. Treat the numbers as examples and build the plan around your own calendar.
