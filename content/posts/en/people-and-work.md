Planning often sits **between people and work**: finding the people you need, dividing up the tasks, aligning the flow. When that process ends in conversation alone, it is easy to repeat the same mistake next time. Once "so who agreed to do what back then?" turns vague, the relationship stays intact while the work tangles.

Even a short note gives you something to answer "how did we do it back then?" later. The record of a connection becomes the blueprint for the next one. And the more stakeholders overlap on a site, the more the **sentence of agreement** matters over the temperature of the tone.

## What I keep in mind when I write

- Separating fact from feeling
- Writing in a way that respects the other person
- Noting one thing that can be used next time
- Adding one line of background so anyone can follow the context
- Deciding in advance whether to name people or only their role

Writing that connects people can easily slide into evaluating people. So I put the focus on "what moved in this situation" rather than "this person is like X." Evaluation is sometimes needed, but even then I leave the grounds as a scene.

## What comes after the connection is the real planning

An introduction or a match is not finished when it is arranged. Whether the first conversation was awkward, whether expectations misaligned, whether a next step appeared — that follow-up raises the accuracy of the next connection. Do not only show the good cases; leave the awkward endings short too. Without a record of failure, a network drifts into vanity.

A connection is a process, not an event. If you leave writing that shows the process, then later, doing something similar, it becomes clear "who you connected, and for what reason." That clarity is also courtesy. If you moved someone's time, you take on the responsibility of remembering that movement.

I am trying to build this blog with the same attitude. Closer to **a continuing record** than to a finished answer. Narrowing the gap between people and work — that, I think, is the writing of a planner who works in the field.
